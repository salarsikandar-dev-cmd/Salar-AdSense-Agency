How this academy works

Rules for becoming an engineer, not just an AI tool operator.

01 · AI-first, engineering-aware

You do not need to become a traditional syntax-first programmer. You do need to understand what the AI is building, inspect generated code, identify mistakes and make informed technical decisions.

02 · Two separate checkmarks

Studied: You completed the learning material and explored the topic.
Learned: You can explain the concept, apply it in a project and answer practical questions without blindly trusting AI.

03 · Build while learning

Every major phase should contribute to a working product, prototype, game, integration or engineering exercise. Do not wait until the end to start building.

04 · Launch, don't just demonstrate

For each suitable product: test it, deploy it, configure its domain, prepare documentation, publish a release and collect feedback. Track real users and actual performance where possible.

05 · Review AI-generated work

Review architecture, dependencies, security, performance, tests and maintainability. A successful build is not proof that the software is correct.

06 · Realistic senior-level target

This curriculum builds the skills and evidence for strong engineering roles. A senior title also requires real production ownership, collaboration, technical judgement, incident handling and experience over time. It cannot be guaranteed by completing lessons alone.

07 · Suggested study schedule

Target: approximately 80 weeks at 20–25 hours per week. A useful weekly split: 25% concepts and research, 45% AI-assisted building, 20% testing and debugging, 10% documentation and review. Adjust based on actual understanding.

08 · Backup your progress

This is a frontend-only tool. Use Export backup regularly to download your progress as JSON. Import that file on another browser or device to restore it.
